Cloverdale, 1895

Ernest Alfred Hall was born on 25 August 1895 at Cloverdale, a 313-acre property near the hamlet of Beech Forest, south of the Otway Ranges.

As a child it seemed he would be a farmer. Then war came to Europe and the British Empire, and one month short of his 20th birthday Ernest caught the train to Melbourne and enlisted in the Australian Imperial Force.

He stood five foot three. His father had a line for that.

“It’s not the size of the dog in the fight, son, but the size of the fight in the dog.”

His father’s words to him

Like so many, Ernest embarked for the war to end all wars. Unlike so many, his letters and records survived. Murray tells his story from them.

In his shoes

Ernest enlisted and embarked for the war. Decades later his great-nephew Murray took his bike to Europe and came to live among the battlefields of the First World War.

About Murray

Murray Ernest Hall grew up in Mentone in the 1950s and 60s and left for Europe at eighteen with his bike. He rode professionally for sixteen years, was twice British National Champion and a multiple Australian Champion, and won two silver medals at the 1974 Commonwealth Games.

Back home he chaired the sport in Western Australia for more than a decade and served Cycling Australia as a national track selector and track commissioner. He later worked in manufacturing, building construction and mining, and now lives on a semi-rural property south-east of Perth.
